# Why mid-February sounds late to a client

SYNTHETIC DEMO CONTEXT. Dates below are **public IRS furnishing calendar** for tax year 2025 / filing season 2026. They are not Quillmere policy and not any real bank's mail schedule.

## Two clocks the client collapses into one

Most people hear one date: "1099s are due January 31."

That is the **usual statutory furnish-to-recipient date** for a long list of standalone information returns (1099-INT, 1099-DIV, 1099-R, and others). When January 31 is a Saturday, Sunday, or federal holiday, the date moves to the next business day.

For 2026:

| What the client thinks | What the calendar actually does |
|---|---|
| "IRS said I should have it January 31." | January 31, 2026 is a Saturday. Standalone recipient copies move to **Monday, February 2, 2026**. |
| "Everyone gets the same form on the same day." | Form **1099-B** (securities proceeds) and any **consolidated brokerage statement** that includes it follow the later **February 15** furnish date. |
| "February 15, then." | February 15, 2026 is a Sunday; February 16 is Presidents' Day. The effective date is **Tuesday, February 17, 2026**. |
| "If I do not have it mid-February, the firm missed the IRS deadline." | Not necessarily. A wealth client with bank interest **and** brokerage activity is often waiting on the **consolidated package**, which is allowed to land later. Paper mail adds days after postmark. |

Electronic filing with the IRS is a third clock (often March 31 for electronic 1099-B / 1099-DIV / 1099-INT). Clients almost never mean that clock. They mean "I want to file" or "my CPA asked me this morning."

## What this does to a contact center

Late January: some clients check early. Language is mild — "is it posted yet," "when do they usually go out."

Mid-February, before the consolidated furnish date: the same intent becomes a complaint. "You are late." "The IRS date already passed." "My neighbor already has theirs." "I cannot file."

A speech-query that only counts "1099" or "tax document" treats both weeks as the same driver. They are not. The mid-February week is **deadline confusion**, not "we failed to mail."

## What last year's page probably said

Typical wealth-firm tax FAQ copy (paraphrased, fictional Quillmere voice):

> Tax documents are mailed by the IRS due date. Allow several days for delivery. Brokerage packages may arrive later.

That sentence is legally cautious and operationally useless. It does not name **February 2 vs February 17**. It does not tell a brokerage household to wait before calling. It does not tell the IVR what to say when the caller already believes the firm is past due.

This repo's memo is the packet you would hand legal/risk to change that sentence and the IVR prompt.
